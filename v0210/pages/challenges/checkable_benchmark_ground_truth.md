# Benchmarks with checkable ground truth

> Equivalence benchmarks whose labels can't be checked cap progress. A refutation benchmark's ground truth grows as models improve.

<p class="tags"><a class="tag" href="#/tags/judge">judge</a><a class="tag" href="#/tags/pairs">pairs</a></p>

## Challenge
Benchmark labels usually can't be checked by the user. EquiBench labels a pair "equivalent" because the tool that generated it is trusted to preserve semantics (compiler passes, a superoptimizer, online-judge verdicts), and the authors concede that toolchain bugs and wrong judge verdicts can mislabel pairs ([EquiBench](#/papers/wei2025equibench "EquiBench: Benchmarking Large Language Models' Reasoning about Program Semantics via Equivalence Checking (2025)"), Limitations). SQLEquiQuest's "equivalent" means equal results on one database the teaching assistants built ([Can the Rookies Cut…](#/papers/singh2024sqlequiquest "Can the Rookies Cut the Tough Cookie? Exploring the Use of LLMs for SQL Equivalence Checking (2024)") §3.1), and SQL-RewriteBench's correctness labels are result consistency on its packaged data ([SQL-RewriteBench](#/papers/long2026sqlrewritebench "SQL-RewriteBench: A Correctness-Gated, Full-Denominator Benchmark for Statement-Level SQL Rewriting (2026)") §5.1), so in our reading its "unsafe" labels are [certificates](#/glossary/certificate) (that data separates the two queries, ties aside) and its "consistent" ones are not. UniQL's authors call agreement on their benchmark databases "strong empirical evidence", not "a complete formal guarantee" ([UniQL](#/papers/gao2026uniql "UniQL: Towards Dialect-Universal Benchmarking for Text-to-SQL (2026)") §8). Build benchmarks whose answers are certificates, so that scores and training rewards can be trusted.

AlphaGo Zero, learning from [self-play](#/glossary/self-play) alone with no human data beyond the rules, surpassed AlphaGo, which had started from human expert games (Silver et al., *Nature* 2016 and 2017). Likewise, a benchmark need not rest on labels its builders fixed in advance when a program can check the answers: a counterexample can be checked by anyone, so every one found, by any method, improves the ground truth.

## Why it matters
- **Scores and rankings rest on the labels.** The SpotIt authors report that searching for a database that separates a BIRD prediction from its [gold query](#/glossary/gold-query), instead of comparing them on the one test database, lowers every method's accuracy and reorders the ranking ([SpotIt](#/papers/klopfenstein2025spotit "SpotIt: Evaluating Text-to-SQL Evaluation with Formal Verification (2026)") §5, Tab. 3), though what survives its search, capped at five rows per table (§5), still counts as correct ([Few Rows Tell Them Apart](#/papers/cohen2026fewrows "Few Rows Tell Them Apart: Equivalence of Queries Mixing Set and Bag Semantics (2026)") §1). They add that when the two disagree, "it is often the gold SQL that is incorrect" (§1; from a sample they labelled themselves, §5, Fig. 5). Pourreza and Rafiei report the opposite error as well: re-judging by hand the Spider dev questions that two top models both failed, they found many of the models' queries correct and some gold queries wrong ([Evaluating Cross-Domain Text-to-SQL Models…](#/papers/pourreza2023evaluating "Evaluating Cross-Domain Text-to-SQL Models and Benchmarks (2023)") §6.2, Tab. 3). Their abstract's "relative performance changes" rests on that selected subset, judged by the authors of one of the two models (§6.2 footnote). Correcting a sampled BIRD Dev subset reordered leaderboard agents ([Pervasive Annotation Errors Break…](#/papers/jin2026annotation "Pervasive Annotation Errors Break Text-to-SQL Benchmarks and Leaderboards (2026)") §5.3, §5.5), mixing corrections with run-to-run variance (answers flip on uncorrected examples too, Fig. 7; our reading).
- **Training data and rewards inherit wrong labels.** Gold SQL in Spider and BIRD training data is often wrong ([SQLDriller](#/papers/yang2025sqldriller "Automated Validating and Fixing of Text-to-SQL Translation with Execution Consistency (2025)") abstract, §2.2, Tab. 1), even though Spider's authors reviewed every mapping by hand (§2.2). The ReViSQL authors, who corrected a sample of BIRD Train, call pervasive annotation errors "the dominant bottleneck" for [RL with verifiable rewards](#/glossary/rl-with-verifiable-rewards-rlvr) on [text-to-SQL](#/glossary/text-to-sql) ([ReViSQL](#/papers/zhu2026revisql "Human-Level Text-to-SQL via Reinforcement Learning on Verified Data, Without Pipeline Engineering (2026)") abstract). A wrong label is a wrong reward, which an optimizer learns to hit ([Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation)).
- **Trusted sources mislabel too.** BIRD keeps a gold query when two annotators' independent queries return identical results on its database ([BIRD](#/papers/li2023bird "Can LLM Already Serve as A Database Interface? A BIg Bench for Large-Scale Database Grounded Text-to-SQLs (2023)") §3.4), which in our reading misses a shared misreading and any difference that database can't show; an audit of BIRD Mini-Dev still found "pervasive annotation errors" ([Pervasive Annotation Errors Break…](#/papers/jin2026annotation "Pervasive Annotation Errors Break Text-to-SQL Benchmarks and Leaderboards (2026)") §3.2, §4.3). The VeriEQL authors found non-equivalent pairs among Calcite's rewrite tests, which are meant to be all equivalent, and the Calcite team confirmed rule bugs among them (home: [Refuting faulty rewrite rules](#/challenges/faulty_rewrite_rules)); they also reported LeetCode problems whose accepted submissions differ from the ground-truth query, and LeetCode acknowledged all of them and fixed most ([VeriEQL](#/papers/he2024verieql "VeriEQL: Bounded Equivalence Verification for Complex SQL Queries with Integrity Constraints (2024)") §6.3). VeriEQL's own bounded "checked" verdict, read as "equivalent", was wrong on some literature pairs inspected by hand (§6.2). In Python, ProbeGen's LLM with execution feedback disproves samples that a benchmark's unit tests accept ([Disproving Program Equivalence with LLMs](#/papers/allamanis2025disproving "Disproving Program Equivalence with LLMs (2025)") §4.2; an estimate resting on an LLM filter). Certified *equivalent* labels need a proof or agreement on a test family that a theorem makes complete for the pair's fragment (home: [Query equivalence: prove or refute](#/challenges/query_equivalence) § Verifiable signal), so their number depends on [SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps).
- **False accepts are the errors that matter.** in self-distillation, held-out gain grows with the checker's precision, while rejecting many correct outputs costs little ([The Verifier is the Curriculum](#/papers/zhou2026curriculum "The Verifier is the Curriculum: Precision Sets the Return on Search in Code Self-Distillation (2026)") § Results; weights trained); random flips of test results barely hurt RL on code, though the study doesn't model a verifier's persistent blind spots ([An Imperfect Verifier is Good Enough](#/papers/plesner2026imperfect "An Imperfect Verifier is Good Enough: Learning with Noisy Rewards (2026)") § Results and § Limits); and a bounded equivalence checker refuted a sizeable share of the rewards a result-match check paid during RL on BIRD ([ReViSQL](#/papers/zhu2026revisql "Human-Level Text-to-SQL via Reinforcement Learning on Verified Data, Without Pipeline Engineering (2026)") § Results); all unchecked, what a certificate buys is precision against systematic false accepts, not a lower error rate as such.
- **Refutation is a hard, checkable task beyond SQL.** The REFUTE authors propose "inverse benchmarks" that test falsifying an incorrect solution rather than solving the problem ([REFUTE ("Can Language Models Falsify?")](#/papers/sinha2025refute "Can Language Models Falsify? Evaluating Algorithmic Reasoning with Counterexample Creation (2025)") §1, §3). Counterexamples are checked by running the incorrect solution against a correct one (§3.2), and they report that top reasoning models find them far less often than they solve the problems, even when given the correct solution (§4.2, §6). Its released evaluator also scores a crash or timeout as a counterexample, and SInQ's gpt-4o-mini harness, which certifies its inequivalent pairs by a diverging input ([Program Semantic Inequivalence Game…](#/papers/micelibarone2025sinq "Program Semantic Inequivalence Game with Large Language Models (2025)") §2.1), counts two identical exceptions as a divergence: the checker needs checking too.
- **Prompt-optimizer research needs such benchmarks** (the comparison challenge itself: [Comparing LLM methods under noise](#/challenges/method_comparison_under_noise)). Studying prompt optimization requires a benchmark "cheap and deterministic to score, hard enough to leave room for improvement, and renewable as models evolve" ([Benchmarking Prompt Optimization of…](#/papers/lesort2026chess "Benchmarking Prompt Optimization of Large Language Models With Chess (2026)") abstract), and a skill-optimization study could not separate a GEPA gain from run-to-run variance with the tasks one repository yields ([Skill Issue](#/papers/kozyrev2026skillissue "Skill Issue: Lessons from Optimizing Repository SKILLs for Coding Agents (2026)") abstract). S3Eval generates SQL-execution items of any length and difficulty, scored against the query's result, and keeps creating unseen ones against leakage ([S3Eval](#/papers/lei2023s3eval "S3Eval: A Synthetic, Scalable, Systematic Evaluation Suite for Large Language Models (2024)") §1, §4.1). In our reading, certified SQL pairs can meet all three needs, and a counterexample task needs no reference answer; the supply of realistic hard pairs is [Sourcing realistic, hard query pairs](#/challenges/query_pair_sourcing)'s problem.

**Size of the gain:** step change, measured as the certified false-accept rate of today's benchmark labels and training rewards (a lower bound, from counterexamples validated by execution), and how scores and rankings change once labels are certificates. Backing every label and reward with a certificate anyone can re-run (a counterexample, or a certificate of equivalence) rather than a generator, a judge's tests or one database makes one error class mostly disappear: credit for answers the check wrongly accepts. The ground truth also grows as models improve instead of staying fixed. The gain shows up in [Text-to-SQL can't be verified against intent](#/challenges/text_to_sql_verification), [Bringing compact models to frontier accuracy](#/challenges/compact_model_accuracy), [Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation) and the evaluation of [Query equivalence: prove or refute](#/challenges/query_equivalence) methods, where a weaker check can reorder methods (SpotIt §5). This is our estimate; certified *equivalent* labels depend on [SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps).

## Verifiable signal
Ask for counterexamples, not verdicts. Every counterexample found is verified, and it improves the ground truth for every method; Kikot et al. turn rows that separate two candidates into "evidence-based criticism of gold answers" ([data-aware NL2SQL candidate selection](#/papers/kikot2026separating "Data-aware candidate selection in NL2SQL translation via small separating instances (2026)") §IV), though which answer is right is still argued informally. Pairs that stay *unknown* are the open frontier: the Polygon authors call the solvability of the pairs VeriEQL left unrefuted "unknown", and checking them by hand "a non-starter" ([Polygon](#/papers/zhao2025polygon "Polygon: Symbolic Reasoning for SQL using Conflict-Driven Under-Approximation Search (2025)") §4). Judge-benchmark authors reach the same conclusion: EquiBench's propose agents that execute and compare programs, since "binary labels alone provide limited learning signals" ([EquiBench](#/papers/wei2025equibench "EquiBench: Benchmarking Large Language Models' Reasoning about Program Semantics via Equivalence Checking (2025)") §6), and LLM-SQL-Solver's propose execution tools against the LLM's hallucinated runs ([LLM-SQL-Solver](#/papers/zhao2023llmsqlsolver "LLM-SQL-Solver: Can LLMs Determine SQL Equivalence? (2025)") §5.2).
