# The self-improving SQL ↔ text ↔ verify loop

> Improve a describer (SQL→English), a writer (English→SQL) and an equivalence checker together, using certified checker verdicts as the reward.

<p class="tags"><a class="tag" href="#/tags/nl2sql">nl2sql</a><a class="tag" href="#/tags/promptopt">promptopt</a></p>

## Challenge
Take Q from a corpus. The describer writes English T, the writer turns T into Q′, and the checker asks whether Q ≡ Q′. A proof rewards the describer and the writer. A counterexample is a verified failure. *Unknown* marks a hard pair and becomes training data for the checker.

```
            ┌───────────── describe ─────────────┐
            │                                    ▼
   SQL query Q  (from a corpus)            English text T
            ▲                                    │
            │                                    │ write
   checker: Q ≡ Q′ ?  ◄──────────────────  SQL query Q′
     ├─ proof            → reward describer + writer
     ├─ counterexample   → verified failure: blame the describer or the writer
     └─ unknown          → hard pair: training data for the checker
```

## What's known
The closest prior work among listed papers is SQL-Zero and SPFT-SQL ("closest" is our judgement). In SQL-Zero ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)")) a challenger writes SQL and then a question for it; a solver writes SQL from the question; [GRPO](#/glossary/grpo) trains both (§3). It has no equivalence checker: the solver is rewarded for matching the challenger's results on a single database (§3.2), so a query that agrees by accident counts as correct, and its admission gate doesn't check that the question asks for what the SQL computes. Its determinism filter: [Equivalence of nondeterministic queries](#/challenges/nondeterministic_equivalence) § Why it matters. SPFT-SQL ([SPFT-SQL](#/papers/zhang2025spftsql "SPFT-SQL: Enhancing Large Language Model for Text-to-SQL Parsing by Self-Play Fine-Tuning (2025)"); SQL-Zero's "closest T2SQL [self-play](#/glossary/self-play)", §2) runs the round trip to synthesize data: SQL from templates of the training queries, a SQL-to-Text model writes the question, the [text-to-SQL](#/glossary/text-to-sql) model writes SQL back, and pairs whose results match on the database train the SQL-to-Text model (§3.1). It too checks results on one database, and adds to annotated pairs (App. A.12); its ablation puts most of the gain in this stage, not the SPIN-style self-play that follows (§3.2, Tab. 2).

On whether gains compound over rounds, listed results differ, under different meanings of "compound" ([The Verifier is the Curriculum](#/papers/zhou2026curriculum "The Verifier is the Curriculum: Precision Sets the Return on Search in Code Self-Distillation (2026)") §2): behind a precise gate, [self-distillation](#/glossary/self-distillation) rounds add up, and a lenient gate erases the gain at a fixed admitted count (abstract, §1); behind a free exact verifier, STaR rounds never accelerate and the base, the authors' own SFT adapter, overtakes the trained model at pass@64 ([Teacher-Free Self-Training Amplifies but…](#/papers/strozzi2026selftraining "Teacher-Free Self-Training Amplifies but Does Not Compound: A Pass@$K$ Crossover on a Free-Verifier Domain (2026)") abstract). Other causes are on record: SQL-Zero's generator loses diversity and its solver's [policy entropy](#/glossary/policy-entropy) falls turn by turn, and its authors leave open which of these or the training budget causes the plateau ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") §6); Yue et al. report that RLVR often narrows the [reasoning boundary](#/glossary/reasoning-boundary) ([Does Reinforcement Learning Really…](#/papers/yue2025rlreasoning "Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model? (2025)") abstract); in self-improvement filtered by the model's own verification, Song et al. report the gain saturating within a handful of rounds and [pass@k](#/glossary/passk) falling at large k, a loss of diversity they tentatively blame on convergence on incorrect answers, which gold labels avoid and a stronger but imperfect verifier doesn't ([Mind the Gap](#/papers/song2024mindgap "Mind the Gap: Examining the Self-Improvement Capabilities of Large Language Models (2025)") §5, App. B.3).

Program optimizers (LLM program optimization) assign credit by a surrogate over module choices ([MIPRO (shipped in DSPy as MIPROv2)](#/papers/opsahlong2024mipro "Optimizing Instructions and Demonstrations for Multi-Stage Language Model Programs (2024)") §3.2), an LLM reading the trace ([GEPA](#/papers/agrawal2025gepa "GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning (2026)") §3), the final reward for every module ([mmGRPO](#/papers/ziems2025mmgrpo "Composing Policy Gradients and Prompt Optimization for Language Model Programs (2026)") §3), or re-running downstream modules with one output fixed ([Optimas](#/papers/wu2025optimas "Optimas: Optimizing Compound AI Systems with Globally Aligned Local Rewards (2026)") §4.1), where an LLM judge does about as well as chance (LLM program optimization).

Still open (ours): a loop that needs no NL–SQL labels and counts only certified agreement; whether its gains compound is our hypothesis, untested.

## Why it matters
- **SQL models trained without NL–SQL labels, on a reward one database can't fool.** The SQL-Zero authors call annotated pairs "a bottleneck for scaling to new databases" ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") abstract), and the ReViSQL authors name annotation errors "the dominant bottleneck for RLVR" on text-to-SQL ([ReViSQL](#/papers/zhu2026revisql "Human-Level Text-to-SQL via Reinforcement Learning on Verified Data, Without Pipeline Engineering (2026)") abstract). SQL-Zero trains with no annotated pairs, but its reward can be fooled (What's known) and its gains don't compound over iterations (§5, §6). The loop needs only a SQL corpus and counts only certified agreement. Whether that makes gains compound is open: that is our hypothesis, and testing it is the loop's first experiment (round_trip_loop_reward; listed results on compounding: What's known). On text-to-SQL accuracy alone we expect incremental gains, of the size SQL-Zero reports over its base ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") Tab. 1; our estimate).
- **Pairs for other challenges.** In our reading, each round trip yields a realistic pair, often a nearly equivalent one: the *unknown* ones show where checkers fail ([SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps)), and the certified ones are labels for [Benchmarks with checkable ground truth](#/challenges/checkable_benchmark_ground_truth) and [Sourcing realistic, hard query pairs](#/challenges/query_pair_sourcing).
- In code, the PSV authors report that removing solution verification from self-play cuts performance sharply ([PSV (Propose](#/papers/wilf2025psv "Propose, Solve, Verify: Self-Play Through Formal Verification (2025)") §6.3, Tab. 2). Their ablation drops the check rather than weakening it, and their proposer writes the spec it is checked against; here Q comes from a corpus.

**Size of the gain:** enabler, our estimate. The quantity it lifts is the number of query pairs certified by a proof or counterexample per corpus query, with no NL–SQL labels, which [SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps), [Benchmarks with checkable ground truth](#/challenges/checkable_benchmark_ground_truth) and [Sourcing realistic, hard query pairs](#/challenges/query_pair_sourcing) draw on. The reason: each round trip yields such a pair for those challenges and evidence for the method, as far as the checker covers enough pairs ([SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps)).

## Why it's hard
This list is the home of the loop's risks; Round-trip consistency links here.
- **Blame assignment and ambiguity:** if several writers fail on the same T, the text is at fault; if other writers succeed, this writer is. The describer errs too: in one qualitative case study, LLM explanations of SQL "often miss or misinterpret key details" ([Evaluating SQL Understanding in…](#/papers/rahaman2024sqlunderstanding "Evaluating SQL Understanding in Large Language Models (2025)") §4.5). In an arithmetic → English → arithmetic round trip decided exactly, [The Communication Bottleneck](#/papers/suau2026bottleneck "The Communication Bottleneck: A Round-Trip Study of Tree-Structured Expression Serialization in Language Models (2026)") reports that most failures start in the describer's text, blamed when no reader in a panel recovers the expression; smaller panels blame the describer more, since a larger one can only move blame to the reader (§2.4, §3.3). Writers certified to disagree with each other mark T as ambiguous, not the writers: the counterexample goes back to the describer, and the count of certified-distinct answers measures T's ambiguity, though not which reading was meant (our reasoning). Text-to-SQL authors know of no automatic way to find ambiguous questions, and models often miss the other readings ([Checking text-to-SQL against intent](#/challenges/text_to_sql_verification) § What's known), so the flag needs different writers, not one resampled (our reasoning). Program optimizers assign credit in several ways (What's known); in our reading only re-running downstream modules with one output fixed measures blame, and here such re-runs are cheap and certified: fix T, run several writers, let the checker score each. An LLM naming the failed stage is fragile: in a statute → logic → English → logic loop, GPT-5.2 blamed the first stage in most iterations "regardless of where the actual error occurs", until the in-order instruction was dropped or Claude diagnosed instead ([Faithful Autoformalization via Roundtrip…](#/papers/amrollahi2026roundtrip "Faithful Autoformalization via Roundtrip Verification and Repair (2026)") §7.5, Tab. 3). An LLM evaluating T on a separating database can rank blame but not certify it ([Checking text-to-SQL against intent](#/challenges/text_to_sql_verification) § Why it's hard, the referee bullet).
- **Joint or per-role optimization:** Coin Flip's ANOVA found no prompt interaction in two-agent feed-forward pipelines ([Prompt Optimization Is a Coin Flip](#/papers/zhang2026coinflip "Prompt Optimization Is a Coin Flip: Diagnosing When It Helps in Compound AI Systems (2026)") §3.2), but its authors predict coupling where outputs feed back or agents exchange code (§7), which is the loop's case (our reading); their pre-test can settle it first.
- **[Reward hacking](#/glossary/reward-hacking):** the describer can paste SQL into the "English"; the RTC authors warn that training on the round-trip objective can teach just that ([Round-Trip Correctness (RTC)](#/papers/allamanis2024roundtrip "Unsupervised Evaluation of Code LLMs with Round-Trip Correctness (2024)") §2). Forbid SQL tokens, cap the length, or have a separate reader check that the text is natural language. Prefer the deterministic guards: in CATCH a chain-of-thought [monitor](#/glossary/monitor-ai-control) used against hacking first suppressed it, then lost its grip as the policy learned to mislead it with code comments ([CATCH](#/papers/wang2026catch "CATCH: A Controllable Analysis Testbed for Reward Hacking in Coding RL (2026)") abstract, §4.3), so a reader must be audited throughout training. More generally, any weak check in the loop gets exploited ([Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation)).
- **Collapse:** the writer can drift to trivially simple queries, and the checker can answer *unknown* everywhere. Reward coverage, not only agreement. A difficulty mechanism the proposer can satisfy trivially will be (Proposer–solver self-play). To see drift, score progress on a frozen, hidden set of certified pairs (Reward auditing). If that set also decides which changes are kept, reusing it lets the search overfit it ([Which Self-Improvements Should We…](#/papers/sun2026reuse "Which Self-Improvements Should We Trust? Reliable Self-Improvement When Agents Reuse Their Benchmarks (2026)") §1); REUSE returns only keep-or-drop decisions and budgets error over their possible histories (abstract, §1).
- **Difficulty rewards pay for ambiguity:** SQL-Zero pays its challenger most when one of five solver samples matches ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") §3.3), so a question that underspecifies its SQL scores as hard (our reasoning). A describer rewarded for hard texts needs the blame rule above.
- **Incomplete checker:** *unknown* means "not proved", not "wrong". Treated as a failure, it punishes correct round trips; paid as a success, it teaches the writer to leave the checker's fragment ([Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation) § What's known, ReViSQL; its § Verifiable signal, PSV).
- **Circularity:** if all three roles use one model, they share blind spots. Mix models and anchor on sound checkers: when GPT-4 checked and critiqued its own answers, the authors found its verification and critique errors compounding until the loop often did worse than its first guess, while a sound verifier raised performance ([On the Self-Verification Limitations…](#/papers/stechly2024selfverification "On the Self-Verification Limitations of Large Language Models on Reasoning and Planning Tasks (2024)") §5, §5.1, §6). In [The Communication Bottleneck](#/papers/suau2026bottleneck "The Communication Bottleneck: A Round-Trip Study of Tree-Structured Expression Serialization in Language Models (2026)")'s round trip the best describer–reader pair mixes two models, above any model reading itself (§3.2).
- **Seed diversity:** gains are limited by how varied the query corpus is.

## Verifiable signal
The checker's *different* verdicts (counterexamples) and its proofs. To tell whether gains compound, coverage against the base counts (pass@k at large k, distinct certified pairs), not only pass@1 (our reasoning, from the results in What's known); the measurement, with the corpus held fixed: round_trip_loop_reward.
