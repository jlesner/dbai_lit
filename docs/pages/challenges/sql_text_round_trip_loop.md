# The self-improving SQL ↔ text ↔ verify loop

> Improve a describer (SQL→English), a writer (English→SQL) and an equivalence checker together, using certified checker verdicts as the reward.

<p class="tags"><a class="tag" href="#/tags/nl2dsl">nl2dsl</a><a class="tag" href="#/tags/promptopt">promptopt</a></p>

## Challenge
Take Q from a corpus. The describer writes English T, the writer turns T into Q′, and the checker asks whether Q ≡ Q′. A proof rewards the describer and the writer. A counterexample is a verified failure. *Unknown* marks a hard pair and becomes training data for the checker.

```
            ┌───────────── describe ─────────────┐
            │                                    ▼
   SQL query Q  (from a corpus)            English text T
            ▲                                    │
            │                                    │ write
   checker: Q ≡ Q′ ?  ◄──────────────────  SQL query Q′
     ├─ proof            → reward describer + writer
     ├─ counterexample   → verified failure: blame the describer or the writer
     └─ unknown          → hard pair: training data for the checker
```

## What's known
The closest listed work, in our judgement, is SQL-Zero and SPFT-SQL. In SQL-Zero ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)")) a challenger writes SQL and then a question for it; a solver writes SQL from the question; [GRPO](#/glossary/grpo) trains both (§3). It has no equivalence checker: the solver is rewarded for matching the challenger's results on a single database (§3.2), so a query that agrees by accident counts as correct (ours), and its admission gate doesn't check that the question asks for what the SQL computes. Its determinism filter: [Equivalence of nondeterministic queries](#/challenges/nondeterministic_equivalence) § Why it matters. SPFT-SQL ([SPFT-SQL](#/papers/zhang2025spftsql "SPFT-SQL: Enhancing Large Language Model for Text-to-SQL Parsing by Self-Play Fine-Tuning (2025)"); SQL-Zero's "closest T2SQL [self-play](#/glossary/self-play)", §2) runs the round trip to synthesize data, from SQL built on templates of the training queries: a SQL-to-Text model writes the question, the [text-to-SQL](#/glossary/text-to-sql) model writes SQL back, and pairs whose results match on the database train the SQL-to-Text model (§3.1). It too checks results on one database, and adds to annotated pairs (App. A.12); its ablation puts most of the gain in this stage, not the SPIN-style self-play that follows (§3.2; §4.5, Tab. 2). Outside SQL, RTRL ([RTRL (Round-Trip Reinforcement Learning)](#/papers/kong2025rtrl "Round-trip Reinforcement Learning: Self-Consistent Training for Better Chemical LLMs (2026)")) trains a chemistry LLM's paired directions (molecule ↔ description, reaction prediction ↔ retrosynthesis, §2) by RL on unlabelled inputs, checked only for format: a frozen copy of the model rewards each output by its likelihood of the original input (§4.1), and in the iterative variant the two directions swap roles after each epoch (§4.2; App. D).

Whether self-improvement gains compound, and why listed loops stall (SQL-Zero's plateau, Zhou's precise and lenient gates, Strozzi's rounds that never accelerate, Song's [pass@k](#/glossary/passk) falling at large k): [Self-improvement that compounds](#/challenges/compounding_self_improvement) § What's known. On math, code and visual reasoning, Yue et al. report that RLVR often narrows the [reasoning boundary](#/glossary/reasoning-boundary) ([Does Reinforcement Learning Really…](#/papers/yue2025rlreasoning "Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model? (2025)") abstract), which others dispute ([RL with verifiable rewards](#/glossary/rl-with-verifiable-rewards-rlvr) (RLVR / GRPO)).

Still open (ours): a loop that needs no NL–SQL labels and counts only certified agreement; whether its gains compound is our hypothesis, untested, and testing it is the loop's first experiment (round_trip_loop_reward).

## Why it matters
- **SQL models trained without NL–SQL labels, on a reward one database can't fool.** The SQL-Zero authors call annotated pairs "a bottleneck for scaling to new databases" ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") abstract), and the ReViSQL authors name annotation errors "the dominant bottleneck for RLVR" on text-to-SQL ([ReViSQL](#/papers/zhu2026revisql "Human-Level Text-to-SQL via Reinforcement Learning on Verified Data, Without Pipeline Engineering (2026)") abstract). SQL-Zero needs no annotated pairs, but its reward can be fooled and its gains don't compound over iterations (What's known; [SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") §5, §6). The loop needs only a SQL corpus and counts only certified agreement. On text-to-SQL accuracy alone we expect incremental gains, of the size SQL-Zero reports over its base ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") Tab. 1; our estimate).
- **Pairs for other challenges.** In our reading, each round trip yields a realistic pair, often a nearly equivalent one: the *unknown* ones show where checkers fail ([SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps)), and the certified ones are labels for [Benchmarks with checkable ground truth](#/challenges/checkable_benchmark_ground_truth) and [Sourcing realistic, hard query pairs](#/challenges/query_pair_sourcing).
- In Verus-verified code, the PSV authors report that removing solution verification from self-play cuts performance sharply ([PSV (Propose](#/papers/wilf2025psv "Propose, Solve, Verify: Self-Play Through Formal Verification (2025)") §6.3, Tab. 2). Their ablation drops the check rather than weakening it, and their proposer writes the spec it is checked against; here Q comes from a corpus.

**Size of the gain:** enabler, our estimate. The quantity it lifts is the number of query pairs certified by a proof or counterexample per corpus query, with no NL–SQL labels, for [SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps), [Benchmarks with checkable ground truth](#/challenges/checkable_benchmark_ground_truth) and [Sourcing realistic, hard query pairs](#/challenges/query_pair_sourcing). The reason: each round trip yields such a pair, and evidence for the method, as far as the checker covers enough pairs ([SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps)).

## Why it's hard
- **Blame assignment and ambiguity:** if several writers fail on the same T, the text is at fault; if other writers succeed, this writer is. The describer errs too: in one qualitative case study, LLM explanations of SQL "often miss or misinterpret key details" ([Evaluating SQL Understanding in…](#/papers/rahaman2024sqlunderstanding "Evaluating SQL Understanding in Large Language Models (2025)") §4.5). In an arithmetic → English → arithmetic round trip decided exactly, [The Communication Bottleneck](#/papers/suau2026bottleneck "The Communication Bottleneck: A Round-Trip Study of Tree-Structured Expression Serialization in Language Models (2026)") reports that most failures start in the describer's text, blamed when no reader in a panel recovers the expression; smaller panels blame the describer more, since a larger one can only move blame to the reader (§2.4, §3.3). Writers certified to disagree mark T as ambiguous, not the writers: the counterexample goes back to the describer, and the number of certified-distinct answers measures T's ambiguity, not which reading was meant (ours). Renggli et al. know of no automatic way to find ambiguous questions whose other readings lack a [gold query](#/glossary/gold-query) ([Fundamental Challenges in Evaluating…](#/papers/renggli2025text2sql "Fundamental Challenges in Evaluating Text2SQL Solutions and Detecting Their Limitations (2025)") §5.2.2), and models often miss the other readings ([Checking text-to-SQL against intent](#/challenges/text_to_sql_verification) § What's known), so the flag needs different writers, not one resampled (ours). Of the program optimizers' ways to assign credit (LLM program optimization), such as mmGRPO's final reward for every module ([mmGRPO](#/papers/ziems2025mmgrpo "Composing Policy Gradients and Prompt Optimization for Language Model Programs (2026)") §3), only re-running downstream modules with one output fixed ([Optimas](#/papers/wu2025optimas "Optimas: Optimizing Compound AI Systems with Globally Aligned Local Rewards (2026)") §4.1) measures blame (ours), and here it is cheap and certified: fix T, run several writers, let the checker score each; in Optimas's tests a prompted LLM judge ranked a module's outputs "closer to random guessing" (§5.2). An LLM naming the failed stage is fragile: in a statute → logic → English → logic loop, GPT-5.2 picked the first stage in most iterations; dropping the prompt's in-order checking cut that share, and Claude as diagnoser raised equivalence ([Faithful Autoformalization via Roundtrip…](#/papers/amrollahi2026roundtrip "Faithful Autoformalization via Roundtrip Verification and Repair (2026)") §7.5, Tabs. 1, 3). An LLM evaluating T on a separating database can rank blame but not certify it ([Checking text-to-SQL against intent](#/challenges/text_to_sql_verification) § Why it's hard; ours).
- **Joint or per-role optimization:** Coin Flip's ANOVA found no significant interaction between two agents' prompts in feed-forward pipelines ([Prompt Optimization Is a Coin Flip](#/papers/zhang2026coinflip "Prompt Optimization Is a Coin Flip: Diagnosing When It Helps in Compound AI Systems (2026)") §3.2), but its authors predict coupling may emerge where outputs feed back or agents exchange code (§7), which is the loop's case (ours); their pre-test can settle it first.
- **[Reward hacking](#/glossary/reward-hacking):** the describer can paste SQL into the "English"; the round-trip correctness authors warn that training on the round-trip objective can teach just that ([Round-Trip Correctness (RTC)](#/papers/allamanis2024roundtrip "Unsupervised Evaluation of Code LLMs with Round-Trip Correctness (2024)") §2). RTRL's authors say its forward model could win its round-trip reward by echoing the input, report seeing this mostly in retrosynthesis, and credit their parser-based format reward with blocking it ([RTRL (Round-Trip Reinforcement Learning)](#/papers/kong2025rtrl "Round-trip Reinforcement Learning: Self-Consistent Training for Better Chemical LLMs (2026)") App. Forbid SQL tokens, cap the length, or have a separate reader check the text is natural language. Prefer the deterministic guards: in CATCH, a coding-RL testbed, a chain-of-thought [monitor](#/glossary/monitor-ai-control) used against hacking first suppressed it, then lost its grip as the policy learned to mislead it with code comments ([CATCH](#/papers/wang2026catch "CATCH: A Controllable Analysis Testbed for Reward Hacking in Coding RL (2026)") abstract, §4.3), so a reader must be audited throughout training. A weak check in the loop can be exploited ([Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation); ours).
- **Collapse:** the writer can drift to trivially simple queries, and the checker can answer *unknown* everywhere. Reward coverage, not only agreement. The proposer may satisfy a difficulty mechanism trivially (Proposer–solver self-play). To see drift, score progress on a frozen, hidden set of certified pairs (Reward auditing). If that set also decides which changes are kept, reusing it lets the search overfit it; REUSE returns only keep-or-drop decisions ([Which Self-Improvements Should We…](#/papers/sun2026reuse "Which Self-Improvements Should We Trust? Reliable Self-Improvement When Agents Reuse Their Benchmarks (2026)") abstract, §1).
- **Difficulty rewards pay for ambiguity:** SQL-Zero pays its challenger most when one of five solver samples matches ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") §3.3), so a question that underspecifies its SQL scores as hard (ours). A describer rewarded for hard texts needs the blame rule above.
- **Incomplete checker:** *unknown* means "not proved", not "wrong". Treated as a failure, it punishes correct round trips; paid as a success, it teaches the writer to leave the checker's fragment ([Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation) § What's known, ReViSQL; its § Verifiable signal, PSV).
- **Circularity:** if all three roles use one model, they share blind spots. In [The Communication Bottleneck](#/papers/suau2026bottleneck "The Communication Bottleneck: A Round-Trip Study of Tree-Structured Expression Serialization in Language Models (2026)")'s round trip the best describer–reader pair mixes two models, above any model reading itself (§3.2).
- **Seed diversity:** the query corpus's variety limits the gains (ours).

This list is the home of the loop's risks; Round-trip consistency links here.

## Verifiable signal
The checker's *different* verdicts (counterexamples) and its proofs. To tell whether gains compound, coverage against the base counts (pass@k at large k, distinct certified pairs), not only pass@1 (ours, from the pass@k results of Yue et al. in § What's known and of Song et al. in [Self-improvement that compounds](#/challenges/compounding_self_improvement) § What's known).
**Named attack (ours):** round_trip_loop_reward, with the corpus held fixed: certified agreement against a one-database reward over a fixed number of rounds; it measures whether the certified arm keeps gaining after the one-database arm stalls.
