# Sourcing realistic, hard query pairs

> Checkers and benchmarks need many query pairs that are *nearly* equivalent. Where do they come from, and how are they labelled?

<p class="tags"><a class="tag" href="#/tags/pairgen">pairgen</a><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/dialect-pairs">dialect-pairs</a></p>

## Challenge
Build a steady supply of diverse query pairs with known or checkable labels, covering the SQL features where tools fail.

## Why it matters
- **Coverage numbers that match real use.** The SQLEquiQuest authors call Calcite's rewrite tests a collection "repurposed to verify that the formal equivalence checking algorithms are indeed behaving as expected", with only equivalent pairs (§3). QED verified none of QO-Verify's LLM rewrites of real enterprise queries ([QO-Verify](#/papers/narasayya2026qoverify "Leveraging Query Optimizers to Verify the Soundness of LLM-based Query Rewrites for Real-World Workloads, and More! (2026)") §5). On SQL-RewriteBench's DBA-enriched pairs, SQLSolver mostly returned unknown or timed out, and VeriEQL couldn't parse or support most ([SQL-RewriteBench](#/papers/long2026sqlrewritebench "SQL-RewriteBench: A Correctness-Gated, Full-Denominator Benchmark for Statement-Level SQL Rewriting (2026)") §7.4). Realistic pairs turn [SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps) into a measured number. Not every checker drops: QO-Verify verifies similar fractions of real and benchmark rewrites (§4.3, Fig. 12).
- **Bugs in the checkers.** Every certified label in [Benchmarks with checkable ground truth](#/challenges/checkable_benchmark_ground_truth) rests on the checkers, and the prover bugs reported so far (Argus, cvc5's authors) are in [Can SQL equivalence checkers be trusted?](#/challenges/checker_soundness); pairs sourced near their rules' edges are what would find more.
- **LLM evaluations that measure reasoning, not a lean or a memory.** On Calcite's equivalent-only pairs, the model that almost never answers "not equivalent" on the other two sets scores best ([Can the Rookies Cut…](#/papers/singh2024sqlequiquest "Can the Rookies Cut the Tough Cookie? Exploring the Use of LLMs for SQL Equivalence Checking (2024)") Tab. 2, §5.1); in our reading, such a set rewards the bias. The SQLEquiQuest (§1, §3.1) and QO-Verify (§1) authors both argue that public benchmark queries have likely been seen in training. On near-miss pairs hand-made from real workloads, the models Rahaman et al. test mostly err by calling non-equivalent pairs equivalent, often where a condition was changed ([Evaluating SQL Understanding in…](#/papers/rahaman2024sqlunderstanding "Evaluating SQL Understanding in Large Language Models (2025)") §3.2, §4.4). Outside SQL, the EquiBench authors report that models lean towards "equivalent" for syntactically similar program pairs and "inequivalent" for dissimilar ones, and conclude that benchmarks should emphasize dissimilar equivalent and similar inequivalent pairs ([EquiBench](#/papers/wei2025equibench "EquiBench: Benchmarking Large Language Models' Reasoning about Program Semantics via Equivalence Checking (2025)") §5.2); [Bringing compact models to frontier accuracy](#/challenges/compact_model_accuracy) needs such pairs.

**Size of the gain:** enabler, measured as the number of realistic, nearly equivalent pairs with a [certificate](#/glossary/certificate) or an honest *unknown*. The gain shows up in [SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps), [Benchmarks with checkable ground truth](#/challenges/checkable_benchmark_ground_truth) and [Bringing compact models to frontier accuracy](#/challenges/compact_model_accuracy); pairs are an input, and much of the supply would come as a by-product of [Discovering new rewrite rules](#/challenges/rewrite_rule_discovery) and the [The self-improving SQL ↔ text ↔ verify loop](#/challenges/sql_text_round_trip_loop). Today realistic pairs cost hand work and lack certified labels: SQL-RewriteBench's DBAs enriched workload queries by hand because many lack a measurable rewrite opportunity (§4.1), and its labels are result consistency on packaged data (§5.1). This is our estimate.

## Verifiable signal
Labels come from the checker: a proof or counterexample, or *unknown* (the hard pairs worth keeping).
