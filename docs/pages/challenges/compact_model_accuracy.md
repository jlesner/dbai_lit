# Bringing compact models to frontier accuracy

> Make a small, cheap model as reliable as a frontier model on checkable SQL tasks, without retraining it (or with only a little training).

<p class="tags"><a class="tag" href="#/tags/llm">llm</a><a class="tag" href="#/tags/promptopt">promptopt</a></p>

## Challenge
Close the gap on SQL synthesis and counterexample discovery, and work out *which* inference-time lever pays off, and when.

## What's known
- **A frontier model can optimize once, offline, for a compact model** that only runs the result: SkillOpt reports larger gains with a frontier optimizer than with one matched to the target in every cell it tests ([SkillOpt](#/papers/yang2026skillopt "SkillOpt: Executive Strategy for Self-Evolving Agent Skills (2026)") §4.3), and GPT-5's instructions, one per question cluster, raised small students' mean on three tasks, the smallest most on MedQA ([Instruction Retrieval at Inference…](#/papers/alkiek2025instructionretrieval "Instruction Retrieval at Inference Time for Small Language Models (2025)") §5), though memory written by larger models gave smaller ones mixed results ([Dynamic Cheatsheet](#/papers/suzgun2025cheatsheet "Dynamic Cheatsheet: Test-Time Learning with Adaptive Memory (2025)") §5), and frontier-written skills lifted smaller executors less than a 27B model's, which the authors explain as frontier procedures assuming "a coding robustness that smaller executors lack" ([Self-Evolving Code-with-Image Reasoning](#/papers/yang2026codewithimage "Self-Evolving Code-with-Image Reasoning (2026)") §5.4).
- **Whether a compact model can optimize for itself is open** (Hyper-reflection); older evidence leans against it (our reading; counter-cases there): as their own optimizer and scorer, Mistral 7B and Llama-2 13B and 70B found instructions that scored below plain chain-of-thought prompts ([Revisiting OPRO](#/papers/zhang2024opro "Revisiting OPRO: The Limitations of Small-Scale LLMs as Optimizers (2024)") §3.2), and smaller models gained less, or inconsistently, from memory they curated themselves ([Dynamic Cheatsheet](#/papers/suzgun2025cheatsheet "Dynamic Cheatsheet: Test-Time Learning with Adaptive Memory (2025)") §4.5). A counter-case where an environment checks what is stored: on ALFWorld, gpt-4o-mini with a database of its own successful trajectories reached about the success rate of the fixed-database agent on gpt-4o, which the authors call "similar" ([Self-Generated In-Context Examples Improve…](#/papers/sarukkai2025selfgenerated "Self-Generated In-Context Examples Improve LLM Agents for Sequential Decision-Making Tasks (2025)") Tab. 2 and caption; ALFWorld only).
- **Structure and step checks can stand in for scale** (our reading): an 8B model whose long-input control flow ran in pre-written combinators about matched the mean of a 70B model writing its own control code ([λ-RLM ("The Y-Combinator for LLMs")](#/papers/roy2026ycombinator "The Y-Combinator for LLMs: Solving Long-Context Rot with $\lambda$-Calculus (2026)") §5.1 C2); a per-step symbolic check with repair lifted 3–4B models far more than frontier ones on long relation chains ([Reason Popper-ly](#/papers/chen2026popper "Reason Popper-ly: Patching In-Context Reasoning with Inductive Logic Programming (2026)") §4, Tab. 1); and with code execution, small thinking models held up on a composition-depth benchmark where small Instruct ones still declined ([Tool Use Reduces Depth-Induced…](#/papers/koplow2026depthcollapse "Tool Use Reduces Depth-Induced Collapse in OOD Reasoning (2026)") Fig. 4).

## Why it matters
- **Queries and data can stay in house.** The LASER authors argue that sending production SQL, schemas and sometimes execution feedback to an LLM API "raises serious privacy and compliance concerns" ([LASER](#/papers/li2026laser "LASER: A Data-Centric Method for Low-Cost and Efficient SQL Rewriting based on SQL-GRPO (2026)") §1). Their 8B and 14B rewriters run locally, on far less GPU memory than the DeepSeek-R1 baselines and at a lower cost than all six baselines in their cost table (§6.3, Tab. 4), though GPT-4o rewrites faster. LASER gets there by training, and it shows the hardware side, not parity: its one-database equivalence rate trails a frontier baseline on two of its five benchmarks ([LASER](#/papers/li2026laser "LASER: A Data-Centric Method for Low-Cost and Efficient SQL Rewriting based on SQL-GRPO (2026)") Tab. 3, Tab. 5).
- **A checker turns cheap samples into answers.** The Monkeys authors report that with repeated sampling, Llama-3-8B exceeds GPT-4o's single-sample score on competitive programming and Lean proofs ([Large Language Monkeys](#/papers/brown2024monkeys "Large Language Monkeys: Scaling Inference Compute with Repeated Sampling (2024)") §1), and that a cheaper open model with several attempts solved more SWE-bench Lite issues than one attempt of GPT-4o or Claude 3.5 Sonnet, at a fraction of the cost (§2.3, Tab. 1); the extra samples spend part of the saving (our reading of Tab. 1). Those scores count a problem as solved when any sample passes tests the model never sees; where tests pass wrong code, Stroebl et al. argue resampling can't lift a weaker model to a sufficiently strong model's single-sample accuracy ([The Limits of Inference…](#/papers/stroebl2024resampling "The Limits of Inference Scaling Through Resampling (2024)") abstract, §3; HumanEval, MBPP; [Best-of-n sampling](#/glossary/best-of-n-sampling) with a verifier).
- Evidence for these methods on compact models, from frontier-optimized prompts to step checks standing in for scale, is in § What's known (Prompt evolution, Hyper-reflection, Memory across problems). A cheap reliable proposer lowers the cost of every run of [Benchmarks with checkable ground truth](#/challenges/checkable_benchmark_ground_truth), [Refuting faulty rewrite rules](#/challenges/faulty_rewrite_rules) and the [The self-improving SQL ↔ text ↔ verify loop](#/challenges/sql_text_round_trip_loop) (our reasoning).

**Size of the gain:** step change, our estimate. It is measured as the hardware a deployment needs to reach a given accuracy on a checkable SQL task. The reason: one local server instead of an API or a large cluster changes where the work can run, not just its price.

## Why it's hard
- **Thinking truncation:** the model reasons until the generation budget runs out and never answers. s1 reports it for a model prompted to reason for many steps, and Oolong's authors for a 4B thinking model on dense long inputs ([Oolong](#/papers/bertsch2025oolong "Oolong: Evaluating Long Context Reasoning and Aggregation Capabilities (2026)") §4.1). The evidence and the guards are in Thinking-budget control.
- Elaborate prompts written by frontier models can derail a small model. In one SEPO run, a self-verification rule added by a 397B architect sent its 8B worker into an "infinite repetition loop", and the next edit removed the rule ([SEPO](#/papers/ma2026sepo "SEPO: Evidence-Grounded Prompt Optimization via Structural Editing (2026)") App. E.2; one worked trace).
- **Prompt optimization needs headroom on both sides.** A model that almost never succeeds gives the search nothing to select on: "the fitness landscape is flat" ([Optimize Cheap](#/papers/oved2026crosstier "Optimize Cheap, Deploy Strong: Cost-Aware Cross-Tier Transfer for Evolutionary Optimization (2026)") §9); ContraPrompt needs "at least one successful retry per training example" ([ContraPrompt](#/papers/rishav2026contraprompt "ContraPrompt: Contrastive Prompt Optimization via Dyadic Reasoning Trace Analysis (2026)") §9); DSPy's bootstrapping assumes "at least a few training examples" pass ([DSPy](#/papers/khattab2023dspy "DSPy: Compiling Declarative Language Model Calls into Self-Improving Pipelines (2024)") §4). At the other end, a seed near the model's prompting ceiling leaves nothing to gain ([RLMOpt](#/papers/satheesha2026rlmopt "RLMOpt: Adaptive Prompt Optimization via Recursive Language Models (2026)") abstract, §6.2; [SAMMO](#/papers/schnabel2024sammo "Symbolic Prompt Program Search: A Structure-Aware Approach to Efficient Compile-Time Prompt Optimization (2024)") §5.1 left GPT-4 out for "negligible headroom"). For SQL counterexample discovery the floor is the risk: if the 8B model rarely finds one, the checker has nothing to reward (our reasoning).
- The gains from each lever (sampling, search, thinking length, prompt optimization) interact and are task- and model-dependent: SAMMO's candidates score with "only weak correlation" across LLMs, so "prompts may need to be optimized separately for each LLM" ([SAMMO](#/papers/schnabel2024sammo "Symbolic Prompt Program Search: A Structure-Aware Approach to Efficient Compile-Time Prompt Optimization (2024)") §5.2); one task "moves from highly optimizable to flat by changing the executor model alone" ([Prompt Optimization Is a Coin Flip](#/papers/zhang2026coinflip "Prompt Optimization Is a Coin Flip: Diagnosing When It Helps in Compound AI Systems (2026)") §5); and "Stronger models do not necessarily improve more" ([Benchmarking Prompt Optimization of…](#/papers/lesort2026chess "Benchmarking Prompt Optimization of Large Language Models With Chess (2026)") §6). Even model order can flip: with a code interpreter, GPT-4o-mini and GPT-3.5 beat GPT-4o on two tasks, which the authors attribute to the larger model choosing text over code ([Steering Large Language Models…](#/papers/chen2024steering "Steering Large Language Models between Code Execution and Textual Reasoning (2025)") §2.2, Figs. 3–4), though over all fourteen tasks GPT-4o leads (Tab. 2); and every model but GPT-4-Turbo extracted web data more accurately by reading each page than through the scraper it wrote ([AutoScraper](#/papers/huang2024autoscraper "AutoScraper: A Progressive Understanding Web Agent for Web Scraper Generation (2024)") §6.1, Tab. 4). Handing the work to an engine can backfire too: routed to logic solvers, 7–8B models scored below their own zero-shot prompting on a mixed set, mostly from solver code that didn't run, and fine-tuning on GPT-4o's code narrowed but didn't close the gap to GPT-4o ([Adaptive LLM-Symbolic Reasoning via…](#/papers/xu2025adaptive "Adaptive LLM-Symbolic Reasoning via Dynamic Logical Solver Composition (2026)") §3.3, Tab. 2 vs Tab. 1). [Self-correction](#/glossary/self-correction) fares worst at equal token cost (our reading): no method tested reliably beat majority voting on Qwen2.5 1.5B–7B in math, and Self-Refine and the authors' forced Reflexion stayed below it even at 7B ([Sample More, Reflect Less](#/papers/mirzaei2026samplemore "Sample More, Reflect Less: Self-Refine and Reflexion Lose to Repeated Sampling at Equal Token Cost, from 1.5B to 7B (2026)") abstract). In code, self-repair often gained modestly once its cost was counted ([Is Self-Repair a Silver…](#/papers/olausson2023selfrepair "Is Self-Repair a Silver Bullet for Code Generation? (2024)") abstract; Best-of-n sampling with a verifier), and more with a stronger model's feedback (§4.2; Agent loop with database and verifier tools).
- **Choosing the lever per question costs compute too.** Compute-optimal scaling needs a per-question difficulty estimate, which the Snell authors derive from many samples per question, call "extremely costly", and leave out of their accounting ([compute-optimal test-time scaling](#/papers/snell2024scaling "Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters (2024)") §3.2, App. C). In SQL, the checker's outcome on a first attempt (a quick counterexample, a timeout, *unknown*) might be a cheaper difficulty signal (our reasoning).
- **RL alone needs a large enough base.** On one 32B base, distilling DeepSeek-R1 beat large-scale RL ([DeepSeek-R1](#/papers/deepseek2025reasoning "DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning (2025)") App. F.1); the authors' early 7B and 16B bases failed to gain meaningfully from pure RL and drifted into repetition as answers grew (App. G.1). On a narrow task it worked at 7B: trained by RL on generated logic puzzles, a 7B model's average on test puzzles of the same family, larger ones included, rose above o1's and DeepSeek-R1's, though not o3-mini-high's ([Logic-RL](#/papers/xie2025logicrl "Logic-RL: Unleashing LLM Reasoning with Rule-Based Reinforcement Learning (2025)") §3, Tab. 2). In the rare language Q, "genuine progress" came only from 14B up, and [GRPO](#/glossary/grpo) typically lowered 1.5B pass rates ([Technical Report](#/papers/hogan2025qlang "Technical Report: Full-Stack Fine-Tuning for the Q Programming Language (2025)") §12, §7.4); RL on a planning task's graph form, then English, let a 1.5B model match zero-shot GPT-4o in English, not in a dialect ([Can Large Language Models…](#/papers/lin2026procedures "Can Large Language Models Generalize Procedures Across Representations? (2026)") §4.1, §4.3).
- **A matching score can hide damage.** 4-bit agents kept their benchmark score while one model's tool-name errors multiplied, because the benchmark tolerates repeated failed calls; a tighter tolerance exposed the gap ([Flat Score, Amplified Failures](#/papers/jang2026flatscore "Flat Score, Amplified Failures: How the Error Budget Masks Damage in Quantized LLM Agents (2026)") § "Introduction"). Parity for a compact model needs per-step checks, not just the final score (our reasoning).

## Verifiable signal
The tasks themselves supply it: counterexamples are checked by execution, and generated examples by execution or a prover. That is what makes compute-for-accuracy trade-offs measurable.
