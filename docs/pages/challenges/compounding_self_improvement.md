# Self-improvement that compounds

> Self-improving LLM loops often gain in the first round, then stall or regress. Make the gains compound: each round adds certified information, and each change is kept only if a certified, statistically controlled check shows it doesn't regress.

<p class="tags"><a class="tag" href="#/tags/harness">harness</a><a class="tag" href="#/tags/promptopt">promptopt</a><a class="tag" href="#/tags/rlvr">rlvr</a></p>

**Source:** added. It joins the compounding claim of [The self-improving SQL ↔ text ↔ verify loop](#/challenges/sql_text_round_trip_loop) to the measurement challenge of [Comparing LLM methods under noise](#/challenges/method_comparison_under_noise).

## Challenge
An improvement loop changes an LLM system's prompts, memory, skills or harness round after round. Many listed loops gain mostly in the first round, then stall or fall (§ What's known). The challenge is to make compounding the rule in prompt space: each round adds certified information (counterexamples, harder items), and each change is kept only when a certified, statistically controlled test shows it doesn't regress.

## What's known
Loops that stall after their first rounds (our reading):
- [Text-to-SQL](#/glossary/text-to-sql) [self-play](#/glossary/self-play) with no human pairs gains mostly in its first iteration in-domain: "no sequence compounds" ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") §5, Tab. 1). Its generator loses diversity and its solver's [policy entropy](#/glossary/policy-entropy) falls turn by turn, and its authors leave open, from single runs, which of these or the training budget causes the plateau (§6).
- STaR self-training against a free, exact verifier on one synthetic DSL, a FlashFill-style "trapdoor" language of string transforms whose verified pairs are cheap to synthesize, gains for two rounds but never accelerates, and the starting model given more tries (pass@64) still beats the trained one ([Teacher-Free Self-Training Amplifies but…](#/papers/strozzi2026selftraining "Teacher-Free Self-Training Amplifies but Does Not Compound: A Pass@$K$ Crossover on a Free-Verifier Domain (2026)") abstract, §3.1, §7; "synthetic domain" is [The Verifier is the Curriculum](#/papers/zhou2026curriculum "The Verifier is the Curriculum: Precision Sets the Return on Search in Code Self-Distillation (2026)")'s word for it, §2); that starting model is the authors' own fine-tuned adapter, not the public Qwen3-4B, by our reading of its tables and code; this may be a property of that verification regime, the authors say (§7). They call the result "amplification, not compounding" (abstract).
- Filtering by the model's own verification saturates within two or three rounds on Qwen-1.5 without new information, falling diversity being a potential cause ([Mind the Gap](#/papers/song2024mindgap "Mind the Gap: Examining the Self-Improvement Capabilities of Large Language Models (2025)") §5, Fig. 4). On GSM8K and MATH, [pass@k](#/glossary/passk) rose over the rounds at small k and fell at large k, a loss of diversity Song et al. tentatively blame on convergence on incorrect answers, which gold labels avoid and a stronger but imperfect verifier doesn't (§5; that ablation on GSM8K with Qwen-1.5-7B, App. B.3).
- Text-to-SQL self-play fine-tuning, retrained on the same data each round, peaks after one iteration ([ExSPIN](#/papers/yan2025exspin "ExSPIN: Explicit Feedback-Based Self-Play Fine-Tuning for Text-to-SQL Parsing (2025)") Tabs. 6–7, §5.8).
- Interpreter-filtered puzzles a code model writes and solves gave "a large benefit from the first iteration and small benefit from the second", a possible plateau to its authors ([Language Models Can Teach…](#/papers/haluptzok2022teach "Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Program Better (2022)") §4.3, Fig. 7; §5).

These five train weights. The same question faces prompt-space loops, the attack the user prefers:
- APE's iterative search shows "diminishing returns" as quality "seems to stabilize after three rounds", on the tasks it plots ([APE](#/papers/zhou2022ape "Large Language Models Are Human-Level Prompt Engineers (2023)") §5.3).
- Where Qwen models rewrite their own instructions from one-sentence seeds on three tasks, Hu et al. report that only a minority of rewrites after the first beat the incumbent, with negative mean held-out effects; Qwen3.5-4B's loops "amount to a single rewrite", though Qwen2.5 loops on TREC "still doubled the gain" after it ([The Winner's Curse in…](#/papers/hu2026winnerscurse "The Winner's Curse in LLM Self-Improvement Loops: Selection Noise, Lock-in, and Acceptance Rules (2026)") §5.1, §5.4, App. C).
- Promptbreeder keeps a diverse population; in one shown run training fitness "continues to increase throughout the run" ([Promptbreeder](#/papers/fernando2023promptbreeder "Promptbreeder: Self-Referential Self-Improvement Via Prompt Evolution (2023)") §1, §5, App. B), though runs stopped once it "appeared to plateau" (App. J.2).
- An agent storing its successful trajectories as examples keeps improving with diminishing returns: "most gains occur within the first 25% of added training tasks" ([Self-Generated In-Context Examples Improve…](#/papers/sarukkai2025selfgenerated "Self-Generated In-Context Examples Improve LLM Agents for Sequential Decision-Making Tasks (2025)") §6.2, Fig. 2).
- In Ctx2Skill's prompt-only self-play, later iterations' skills scored below the first's on GPT-4.1, "adversarial collapse" to its authors ([From Context to Skills](#/papers/si2026ctx2skill "From Context to Skills: Can Language Models Learn from Context Skillfully? (2026)") §3.4, §4.3, Tab. 3); one run per setting (App.
- In SkillLearnBench's four-round runs (one category, one solver), self-revised skills' accuracy rose once, then fell sharply, while hints from a teacher who sees the human-written skill raised it every round: "external feedback gives the model something new to learn from" ([SkillLearnBench](#/papers/zhong2026skilllearnbench "SkillLearnBench: Benchmarking Continual Learning Methods for Agent Skill Generation on Real-World Tasks (2026)") §3.3, §5.4, Fig. 4).

Loops that keep gaining mostly share a precise check on what is kept, a gate against regressions, and new information each round (our reading):
- In [self-distillation](#/glossary/self-distillation) by rejection-sampling fine-tuning, held-out gain grows with the checker's precision, rejecting correct outputs costs little (with [GRPO](#/glossary/grpo) in its place, false rejections push the policy away from [rollouts](#/glossary/rollout) that passed), and with a precise gate three rounds compound ([The Verifier is the Curriculum](#/papers/zhou2026curriculum "The Verifier is the Curriculum: Precision Sets the Return on Search in Code Self-Distillation (2026)") §8, Figs. 1 and 5; §6, Tab. 1; weights trained, one engine). That gate asks only whether a generated Godot game project launches cleanly in a headless engine: behind it, rounds add up on held-out game families, and swapping only it for a lenient build check erases the gain at a fixed admitted count (abstract, §1, §7). Whether self-training reaches beyond its base, which Strozzi et al. answer no (above), is "a distinct question" from theirs, Zhou et al. say (§2): listed results on compounding differ partly because they mean different things by it (ours).
- Re-optimizing on new Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks, the authors' own optimizer, which rejects any change breaking an already-solved task, averaged highest; a GEPA-optimized prompt of lessons first fell below the unoptimized agent ([Do Agent Optimizers Compound?](#/papers/wang2026compound "Do Agent Optimizers Compound? A Continual-Learning Evaluation on Terminal-Bench 2.0 (2026)") §2.5, §6.4, Tab. 7; the fall: [Gains made against a checker, carried beyond it](#/challenges/transfer_beyond_the_checker) § Why it's hard).
- Harness edits kept only when an LLM ties a gain on some verification task, and no regression, to the edit, and the edit then wins on a mostly fresh batch, never fell below the unedited harness in Tab. 1's twelve held-out results ([HarnessLens ("Verify Smarter](#/papers/xu2026harnesslens "Verify Smarter, Evolve Further: Efficient Harness Evolution through Behavior-Aware Verification (2026)") §5.2; the gate: App. A.4.1–A.4.2).
- Over five rounds of skill self-evolution on Terminal-Bench 2 with one backbone, admitting every skill rose to a peak and fell back while VaG's pre-commit gate (three critics, marginal-gain subset selection) rose every round, its authors report, both scored on the skills' source tasks, "an optimistic upper bound" ([VaG ("When Self-Evolution Backfires")](#/papers/shang2026vag "When Self-Evolution Backfires: Pre-Commit Gating against Skill Contamination in LLM Agents (2026)") § "Experiments", Tab. 1).
- Averaged over 12 runs, SkillGLoW's gated library rose through round 3 while each round's candidate, admitted unconditionally, fell after round 1, its authors report; the gate scores on the training stream ([SkillGLoW (Global–Local Weave)](#/papers/yan2026skillglow "SkillGLoW: Procedural-Family Skill Consolidation for Self-Improving Agents on Long-Horizon Task Streams (2026)") §4.3, Fig. 3; App. D.3).
- With no regression gate, DGM, a [coding agent](#/glossary/coding-agent) that edits its own code and archives every version that compiles and still edits code, improved over 80 iterations in its authors' runs, while always editing the latest agent "makes very little progress" on SWE-bench ([Darwin Gödel Machine (DGM)](#/papers/zhang2025dgm "Darwin G\'odel Machine: Open-Ended Evolution of Self-Improving Agents (2026)") §3, §4.4, App. A.1; one run each); the task subsets that score it also weight parent selection (§3, §4.2; ours).
- A proof agent rewriting its own prompts, tools and workflow against a fixed checker, on a practice set that swaps solved problems for new or harder ones, reached a higher held-out solve rate than on a fixed set, though not monotonically ([Self-Modifying Lean Proof Agents](#/papers/li2026selfmodifying "Self-Modifying Lean Proof Agents with Verifier-Grounded Benchmark Coevolution (2026)") §4.1–4.2, Tab. 1; one run).
- [Expert iteration](#/glossary/expert-iteration) in theorem proving "plateaus" because correct proofs of the unproved statements are too rare, STP's authors say, while its self-generated conjectures, proved more often, give "more training signals, leading to better scaling" ([STP](#/papers/dong2025stp "STP: Self-play LLM Theorem Provers with Iterative Conjecturing and Proving (2025)") §4.4; weights trained).
- A data-bootstrapping loop for q stalled when the remaining problems were too hard, resumed after pretraining on q code, then plateaued again ([Technical Report](#/papers/hogan2025qlang "Technical Report: Full-Stack Fine-Tuning for the Q Programming Language (2025)") §3.1.1–3.1.2, Figs. 2, 4; weights trained); its first run's gains were "largely illusory", from tests written with the solutions (§3.1.1; [Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation) § What's known).
- CwI switches from reading failures to re-running them once its probe score stalls, and its ablation puts part of the uplift on that step ([Self-Evolving Code-with-Image Reasoning](#/papers/yang2026codewithimage "Self-Evolving Code-with-Image Reasoning (2026)") §4.2, §5.5, validation split); its delivery gate is "not a guarantee against test regression" (§4.3).
- New verified examples targeting each failure pattern raise the gains of three prompt optimizers on three tasks ([FORGE](#/papers/yuan2026forge "Failure-Guided Co-Evolution of Prompts and Training Data (2026)") § "Transferability of Synthesized Data", Tab. 4; an LLM verifies): in our reading, new information of this kind is one way to avoid the stall.

## Why it matters
- **Compounding separates a method from a system that keeps getting better.** A loop that gains once is one more method; one whose per-round gain stays above zero keeps improving with no human labels or weight training (our reasoning).
- **Every self-improvement loop needs the gate.** Reusing one evaluation set for keep-or-reject decisions promotes false improvements; REUSE's rule against it ([Which Self-Improvements Should We…](#/papers/sun2026reuse "Which Self-Improvements Should We Trust? Reliable Self-Improvement When Agents Reuse Their Benchmarks (2026)")), which in its authors' live runs made none, on one tabular task: [Comparing LLM methods under noise](#/challenges/method_comparison_under_noise) § Why it matters. Scored against one reference program, an evolving test search looked like progress ([Auditing and Decomposing Feedback-Driven…](#/papers/liang2026testgen "Auditing and Decomposing Feedback-Driven Evolution in LLM Test Generation under the Oracle Problem (2026)") §4.2). Noise is [Comparing LLM methods under noise](#/challenges/method_comparison_under_noise)'s side of this, bias [Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation)'s. Guarding against gaming costs: in a controlled prompt-evolution host, only one of HackProbe's three protection levels returned more true capability under hacking than it cost on clean runs, by a ratio a larger audit set could move, its authors say ([HackProbe](#/papers/yang2026hackprobe "Harness-agnostic detection and immunization of reward hacking in self-evolving language models (2026)") §4, §4.3, Tabs. 2–3).
- **Prompt-space learning works when it is checked.** A frozen model that keeps a tip only when the tip then solves its problem beats GEPA, RL and memory baselines in most task-and-size conditions ([CORE (Contrastive Reflection)](#/papers/nasvytis2026core "CORE: Contrastive Reflection Enables Rapid Improvements in Reasoning (2026)") §3, §4.3, Tab. 1). Stored verified corrections raise held-out text-to-SQL accuracy on the same databases; stored unchecked majority-vote answers fall below no memory ([Measuring Crystallization in Text-to-SQL](#/papers/wang2026crystallization "From Test-Time Scaling to Reusable Memory: Measuring Crystallization in Text-to-SQL (2026)") §5.1, Tab. 1; §5.3, Tab. 18). In text games, context optimization with an insight memory is competitive with RL on the one model run with RL ([MEMO](#/papers/xie2026memo "MEMO: Memory-Augmented Model Context Optimization for Robust Multi-Turn Multi-Agent LLM Games (2026)") §5 Obs. 1, Tab. 2). The admission check: [Checking a memory across problems before it is reused](#/challenges/verified_memory).
- **SQL supplies the [certificates](#/glossary/certificate).** A counterexample validated by execution, or a proof, gives a precise gate and new information each round (ours).

**Size of the gain:** step change, our estimate. Measured as held-out certified accuracy per round of an improvement loop, where compounding means the per-round gain stays above zero past round three. The reason: a system would keep improving without labels or weight training; contested (our reading), since many listed loops gain mostly in one round, several that compound train weights or score on their own training or selection data (Zhou, STP, VaG, SkillGLoW, DGM), and the held-out self-modifying case is one run ([Self-Modifying Lean Proof Agents](#/papers/li2026selfmodifying "Self-Modifying Lean Proof Agents with Verifier-Grounded Benchmark Coevolution (2026)") App. A) (§ What's known).

## Why it's hard
- **A sound gate can stall the loop.** REUSE promoted far fewer changes than the ungated rules, though its final held-out gain was comparable to the best ([Which Self-Improvements Should We…](#/papers/sun2026reuse "Which Self-Improvements Should We Trust? Reliable Self-Improvement When Agents Reuse Their Benchmarks (2026)") abstract, Tab. 1); over a longer loop a strict gate may reject nearly everything (ours).
- **A score gate passes whole updates.** In RSE-Bench's case studies, rules learned from misbound outcomes passed a validation gate into the deployed skill store; validation judges each update as a whole, not its clauses ([When Non-Adversarial Noise Hurts Learning](#/papers/yu2026rsebench "When Non-Adversarial Noise Hurts Learning: Benchmarking Skill Self-Evolution in Agents (2026)") §5.4, App. G.4–G.5).
- **A no-regression gate can lift in-domain without lifting held-out task types:** GRASP's gate, given to five baselines with one proposal per batch on one benchmark and model, lifted each in-domain and none on held-out task types ([GRASP](#/papers/moll2026grasp "GRASP: Gated Regression-Aware Skill Proposer for Self-Improving LLM Agents (2026)") §4.6, Tab. 5). Gate variants: Statistical acceptance gate.
- **A no-regression gate may block the changes that gain most** (ours): structural changes can break some solved tasks before they pay off, and a step-attributing optimizer gained most where it changed the pipeline ([FAPO](#/papers/kassianik2026fapo "FAPO: Fully Automated Prompt Optimization of Multi-Step LLM Pipelines (2026)") §4.3, Tab. 2), though it doesn't measure what those changes broke.
- **Regressions are hard to attribute.** Per-instance outcomes change between identical agent runs ([SWE-agent](#/papers/yang2024sweagent "SWE-agent: Agent-Computer Interfaces Enable Automated Software Engineering (2024)") App. B.5), so a task that flips after an edit may not be the edit's doing (our reading).
- **Headroom shrinks with stronger models:** evolved text-to-SQL prompts and tools gain most for the cheapest model on BIRD dev ([RoboPhD](#/papers/borthwick2026robophd "RoboPhD: Self-Improving Text-to-SQL Through Autonomous Agent Evolution (2026)") §4.2, Tab. 1).
- **Prompt-space memory overloads.** Merging many reflections in one call drops specific lessons ([Combee](#/papers/li2026combee "Combee: Scaling Parallel Prompt Learning for Self-Improving LLM Agents (2026)") §2.2, Fig. 2). In one AppWorld run, success fell beyond 90 sessions as DAEDALUS's bank of validated heuristics grew, and hierarchical consolidation did not recover it, its authors report ([DAEDALUS](#/papers/edy2026daedalus "DAEDALUS: Bootstrapping Agent Memory from Self-Generated Tasks (2026)") §5.1, Fig. 6).
- **Scaffold gains may not outlast the next model:** one generated prompt on a newer model beat many published ICSE 2026 LLM-based software-engineering techniques ([What Survives the Next Model?](#/papers/salsabil2026survives "What Survives the Next Model? Benchmarking LLM-Based Techniques Against Single-Prompts (2026)") abstract, §V-A; [Whole-job reliability of multi-step LLM work](#/challenges/whole_job_reliability) § Why it's hard).
- **The checker limits the loop.** In SQL, the share of realistic pairs that checkers support can be small ([SQL features that verifiers don't cover](#/challenges/verifier_coverage_gaps)), so certified verdicts may come mostly from easier items (ours).

## Verifiable signal
Held-out certified accuracy after each round, on items never used for selection, with the keep-or-reject rule fixed in advance, against an ungated and a self-judged loop, over five or more rounds and several seeds. The loop compounds when the per-round gain stays above zero, beyond the test's error, past round three. The gate's measure is its false-promotion rate on known-null changes. In SQL, executed counterexamples and proofs certify items and verdicts; rerunning the stored certificates ([counterexample databases](#/glossary/counterexample-database) and proofs of earlier pairs) after an edit names each pair whose certified verdict the edited system now gets wrong, exactly what the edit broke, the list AHE's agent couldn't predict ([Checking a memory across problems before it is reused](#/challenges/verified_memory) § What's known) (ours). The statistics: [Comparing LLM methods under noise](#/challenges/method_comparison_under_noise) § Verifiable signal.
**Named attack (ours):** a prompt-space loop on SQL whose certified per-round gain stays positive past round three; it measures that gain together with the gate's false-promotion rate.
