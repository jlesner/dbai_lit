# Comparing LLM methods under noise

> Reported gains among prompts, optimizers, verifiers and training recipes often sit inside run-to-run and small-sample noise, or are scored on the set that picked the winner. Certified, renewable test items, SQL pairs or the DSL gym's code-made tasks, make the comparison cheap to redo.

## Challenge
Which LLM methods make models reliable on checkable problems is answered by comparisons. The challenge is to compare methods so that a reported gain lies outside run-to-run and small-sample noise, is scored on items never used to pick the winner, comes from rivals run in one setup at equal cost, and measures what it claims to. The cited papers fail in five recurring ways, mostly in prompt optimization (ours; § What's known).

## What's known
- **Gains inside the noise.** At the dataset size one repository supplies, a skill-optimization study finds its GEPA gain "cannot be separated from the agent's run-to-run variance", and settling that "would take more tasks than one repository's history yields" ([Skill Issue](#/papers/kozyrev2026skillissue "Skill Issue: Lessons from Optimizing Repository SKILLs for Coding Agents (2026)") abstract). RLMOpt's authors treat differences within the per-run [standard error](#/glossary/standard-error) as ties and call their margins over GEPA "generally comparable" to it ([RLMOpt](#/papers/satheesha2026rlmopt "RLMOpt: Adaptive Prompt Optimization via Recursive Language Models (2026)") §4, §7). SEPO's authors re-ran five tasks with "high run-to-run variance" on three more paired seeds against GEPA, with a Llama worker, and SEPO's mean stayed higher on each ([SEPO](#/papers/ma2026sepo "SEPO: Evidence-Grounded Prompt Optimization via Structural Editing (2026)") App. G.1, Tab. 9); on most of them, though, the added seeds went either way and the mean gap is smaller than either method's standard deviation across runs (Tab. 9, our reading). Coin Flip's authors find that question difficulty explains far more variance "than any prompt effect" ([Prompt Optimization Is a Coin Flip](#/papers/zhang2026coinflip "Prompt Optimization Is a Coin Flip: Diagnosing When It Helps in Compound AI Systems (2026)") §3.2). Greedy decoding is no floor: with the same seeds and greedy sampling, s1's scores "can change significantly across runs" ([s1](#/papers/muennighoff2025simple "s1: Simple test-time scaling (2025)") App. B), and batched serving keeps greedy decoding "only approximately reproducible" ([Measuring Crystallization in Text-to-SQL](#/papers/wang2026crystallization "From Test-Time Scaling to Reusable Memory: Measuring Crystallization in Text-to-SQL (2026)") §4). ArrivalBench's author withdrew three pilot claims that did not replicate at the full task count, among them "that hints do not help", and pre-registers the analysis for that reason ([ArrivalBench](#/papers/kothari2026arrivalbench "ArrivalBench: Agent-Generated Data Pipelines Are Correct Once and Wrong Under Time (2026)") §4.1, App. B). Some meaning-preserving formatting rewrites alone moved code models' accuracy by more than the margin some advances are claimed with ([TokDrift](#/papers/li2025tokdrift "TokDrift: When LLM Speaks in Subwords but Code Speaks in Grammar (2025)") §4.1).
- **The selection set is the reported set.** Picking among several candidates on a small validation set "is a multiple-testing problem", in which a candidate may rank first by noise ([ESPO](#/papers/liu2026espo "ESPO: Error-Structured Prompt Optimization via Diagnose, Diversify, and Stabilize (2026)") §3.4), and the winner is often reported on that same set: LSE reports the best holdout score reached over its rounds ([Learning to Self-Evolve (LSE)](#/papers/chen2026lse "Learning to Self-Evolve (2026)") App. A); Adaptive-GEPA's "final selection reuses the same validation set" ([Adaptive-GEPA](#/papers/chen2026adaptivegepa "Adaptive-GEPA: Make Your Harness Fit Heterogeneous Requests (2026)") App. L); FLARE's Table 1 reports each method's best test score across a sweep, though its §3 says the test set "never informs prompt selection" ([FLARE](#/papers/sundararaman2026flare "FLARE: Few-shot Learning-based Adaptive Reflective Engine (2026)") Tab. 1–2, §3; our reading); in ADAS's released runs, each reported agent is its archive's best on the test set ([ADAS](#/papers/hu2024adas "Automated Design of Agentic Systems (2024)") Tab. 1 and released archives; ours). Early optimizers did it too: ProTeGi reports test F1 "based on maxpooling over the final beam of candidates" ([ProTeGi](#/papers/pryzant2023protegi "Automatic Prompt Optimization with 'Gradient Descent' and Beam Search (2023)") §3.2; our reading), and EvoPrompt's BBH development set is "a subset from the test set" ([EvoPrompt](#/papers/guo2023evoprompt "EvoPrompt: Connecting LLMs with Evolutionary Algorithms Yields Powerful Prompt Optimizers (2024)") §4.4). A test-time memory is scored on the task stream that builds it, with the benchmark's own tests as its feedback ([Learning from Execution](#/papers/li2026privatelibrary "Learning from Execution: Self-Evolving Memory for Private-Library Code Generation (2026)") §VI-B, §VII-A; our reading). In RL, Uesato et al. "select the best model of the 5, based on final-answer test error" ([process- vs outcome-based feedback](#/papers/uesato2022process "Solving math word problems with process- and outcome-based feedback (2022)") §2.6). An optimizer shown the selection items can memorize them (Prompt evolution and Critique-and-edit prompt refinement).
- **Unequal comparisons.** Rivals run each other's baselines in their own setups: ReSequel attributes gaps to earlier numbers to "the underlying LLMs … indicating strong sensitivity" ([ReSequel](#/papers/fathollahzadeh2026resequel "ReSequel: Robust LLM-assisted Query Rewriting and Optimization using Templatization and Sampling (2026)") §7.2), and LASER represents E3-Rewrite by a naive-GRPO model it trained itself ([LASER](#/papers/li2026laser "LASER: A Data-Centric Method for Low-Cost and Efficient SQL Rewriting based on SQL-GRPO (2026)") §6.1.3). Extra tokens raise accuracy by themselves, Mirzaei et al. argue, so they score each method against repeated sampling at its own measured cost ([Sample More, Reflect Less](#/papers/mirzaei2026samplemore "Sample More, Reflect Less: Self-Refine and Reflexion Lose to Repeated Sampling at Equal Token Cost, from 1.5B to 7B (2026)") abstract).
- **The metric measures something else.** NPO's authors found formatting errors were "a nontrivial fraction of observed failures" that "could therefore distort the comparison between optimization methods" ([Naive Prompt Optimization (NPO)](#/papers/chang2026npo "Naive Prompt Optimization: Rethinking the Need for Complex Prompt Search (2026)") §2.4). On BIRD, rankings change when scoring searches for a counterexample instead of using one test database ([Benchmarks with checkable ground truth](#/challenges/checkable_benchmark_ground_truth) § Why it matters), and, for prompting methods on its financial domain, when gold queries are corrected ([Understanding the Effects of…](#/papers/wretblad2024noise "Understanding the Effects of Noise in Text-to-SQL: An Examination of the BIRD-Bench Benchmark (2024)") §5, a single evaluation). Scored against one accepted program, evolved tests' gain was inflated ([Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation) § What's known); after an audit by the other accepted programs, independent sampling of LLM-written tests beat [evolutionary search](#/glossary/evolutionary-search) at equal candidate slots in every model and cohort, while evolution's edge over one-shot generation was small or absent ([Auditing and Decomposing Feedback-Driven…](#/papers/liang2026testgen "Auditing and Decomposing Feedback-Driven Evolution in LLM Test Generation under the Oracle Problem (2026)") §4.3). On released 7B base–RL pairs, most of the out-of-domain multiple-choice gain that harness extractors reported was answer format once one fixed extractor read both models' outputs ([Same Answer, New Box](#/papers/lyu2026sameanswer "Same Answer, New Box: How Much of RLVR's Out-of-Domain Gain Is Answer Format? (2026)") §4.1). Re-extracting and renaming a weak code model's function, with no new logic, raised its hidden-test passes ([Selection Without Signal, Recovery…](#/papers/iscan2026falsification "Selection Without Signal, Recovery Through Expression: A Measurement Study of Post-Hoc Falsification Operators for Frozen Small Code Models (2026)") §3, Tab. 4), so a fix of syntax alone can read as a gain unless unparsable, crashing and wrong programs are scored apart (ours). Scoring each arm on its own task set can create, hide or exaggerate a difference ([Quantization Effects on Tool-Failure…](#/papers/hu2026toolrecovery "Quantization Effects on Tool-Failure Recovery Vary Across Prompts and Evaluation Designs (2026)") §5.2), and so can mixing greedy and sampled decoding ([Prompt Dominance and Asymmetric…](#/papers/hou2026grpo "Prompt Dominance and Asymmetric Verifier Costs: Empirical Ablations of GRPO at 1B Scale on GSM8K (2026)") §2, whose own §3.4 mixes them). A gain under a reward need not come from the reward ([Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation) § What's known: random-reward baselines).
- **Baselines that don't reproduce.** Two later groups' re-runs fell short of most of TextGrad's reported numbers, one on a new split (Critique-and-edit prompt refinement). Hochlehnert et al.'s re-run in one seed-averaged setup, with paired tests against its base model, found no statistically significant gain for OpenRS despite its reported ones, and found scores move with hardware and evaluation software even with seeds fixed ([A Sober Look at…](#/papers/hochlehnert2025sober "A Sober Look at Progress in Language Model Reasoning: Pitfalls and Paths to Reproducibility (2025)") §4.2–4.3, §3.4).
- **Tests in use.** The papers already use most parts of the verifiable signal below: a [sign test](#/glossary/sign-test) over the tasks where a candidate's and the seed's [rollouts](#/glossary/rollout) disagree ([Skill Issue](#/papers/kozyrev2026skillissue "Skill Issue: Lessons from Optimizing Repository SKILLs for Coding Agents (2026)") §4.2), [McNemar's exact test](#/glossary/mcnemars-exact-test) on paired items, read as "bounded, directional evidence" ([SQL-Zero](#/papers/pedrozo2026sqlzero "SQL-Zero: Self-Evolving Text-to-SQL (2026)") §5), a paired bootstrap ([LLMs as a Jury](#/papers/liu2026jury "LLMs as a Jury: Cross-Model Consensus Can Outperform Process Reward Models for LLM Reasoning (2026)") App. A) or a two-stage one ([Measuring Crystallization in Text-to-SQL](#/papers/wang2026crystallization "From Test-Time Scaling to Reusable Memory: Measuring Crystallization in Text-to-SQL (2026)") §4), matched instances across methods ([Naive Prompt Optimization (NPO)](#/papers/chang2026npo "Naive Prompt Optimization: Rethinking the Need for Complex Prompt Search (2026)") §2.4), a post-hoc placebo arm that nearly reproduces an apparent feedback effect ([Auditing and Decomposing Feedback-Driven…](#/papers/liang2026testgen "Auditing and Decomposing Feedback-Driven Evolution in LLM Test Generation under the Oracle Problem (2026)") §4.6), and acceptance gates inside the search (Statistical acceptance gate). A claim of no loss needs a margin fixed in advance: [When the Reward Suite Is Leaky](#/papers/zhang2026leaky "When the Reward Suite Is Leaky: A Preregistered Causal Contrast of Natural Verifier False Positives in RLVR (2026)") pre-registered its non-inferiority margin, takes the seed as the unit of inference, and prices one axis of its own analysis freedom with family-adjusted [p-values](#/glossary/p-value) (§3.3, §3.6, §4.1, §4.4). Fresh items can be made per decision: SkillSandbox synthesizes tasks that exercise a skill and compares runs with and without it; with one model, its verdicts tracked held-out effects better than source-task re-runs or random tasks did ([SkillSandbox](#/papers/kim2026skillsandbox "SkillSandbox: Skill Verification via Dynamic Scenario Synthesis (2026)") abstract, §5.1; no repeated runs). None of these papers measures an A/A floor for a prompt optimizer's comparisons (ours); the first experiment for it is loop_gain_noise_floor, which scores one fixed arm against itself on gym tasks.

## Why it matters
- **Selection turns noise into false gains.** Picking the best of several noisy scores overstates the winner's gain, Hu and Tang show under their model's assumptions ([The Winner's Curse in…](#/papers/hu2026winnerscurse "The Winner's Curse in LLM Self-Improvement Loops: Selection Noise, Lock-in, and Acceptance Rules (2026)") §3.1). Reusing one selection set over many rounds compounds that noise (§ What's known), as later candidates adapt to it: REUSE's authors report false promotions by current self-improvement systems' rules, AlphaEvolve-inspired population searches among them, judged on a held-out estimate. Their own rule returns only decisions to the search and budgets its error over the possible promotion histories, so it certifies promotions on the reused set, and in their live runs it made none, though the smallest gain it can detect grows as rounds and promotions add up ([Which Self-Improvements Should We…](#/papers/sun2026reuse "Which Self-Improvements Should We Trust? Reliable Self-Improvement When Agents Reuse Their Benchmarks (2026)") abstract, §2.1–2.2, §3.1–3.2, Tab. 1; one tabular task; its live runs' procedure is undescribed). Where Qwen models rewrite instructions from four failures at a time, in a pre-registered study of greedy (keep-if-better) loops, the final selection-set score overstated held-out accuracy far more with 16 selection items than with 256 ([The Winner's Curse in…](#/papers/hu2026winnerscurse "The Winner's Curse in LLM Self-Improvement Loops: Selection Noise, Lock-in, and Acceptance Rules (2026)") §5, §5.3). In the same paper's GEPA runs in DSPy, with a Qwen3.5-4B task model on TREC, Banking77 and GSM8K, so did the validation score GEPA selects on, for the held-out gain, with 16 validation items far more than with 256, while the held-out gain itself did not grow with more validation items; the authors measure that score, not GEPA's published test results (§5.4, Fig. 3). Dwork et al. note that a reused holdout can overfit to itself, as when only the best of many tested predictors is reported, and that static multiple-testing corrections don't apply once the choices are adaptive. Their Thresholdout provably validates many adaptively chosen queries on i.i.d. data as long as few of them overfit ([Generalization in Adaptive Data…](#/papers/dwork2015holdout "Generalization in Adaptive Data Analysis and Holdout Reuse (2015)") §1, §4.1); in their no-signal synthetic data, a standard reused holdout reported accuracy well above chance (§5).
- **Certified items can supply the items the papers lack** (§ Verifiable signal; the argument's home: [Benchmarks with checkable ground truth](#/challenges/checkable_benchmark_ground_truth) § Why it matters).

**Size of the gain:** enabler (our estimate). It lifts the share of claimed method gains in [Prompt-space self-play judged by an engine](#/challenges/prompt_space_self_play), [Self-improvement that compounds](#/challenges/compounding_self_improvement), [Reliability that holds with size, by handing the procedure to an engine](#/challenges/engine_offloading_with_size), [Bringing compact models to frontier accuracy](#/challenges/compact_model_accuracy) and the [The self-improving SQL ↔ text ↔ verify loop](#/challenges/sql_text_round_trip_loop) that survive re-scoring on fresh certified items under a paired test fixed in advance. Every claim in those challenges is a comparison.

## Why it's hard
- The noise has sources the papers mix: which items were drawn, the executor's run-to-run variance (even greedy, § What's known), the optimizer's own randomness, a stochastic judge, and selection itself. Tests over seeds miss the first; tests over items miss the second and third (our reading). Paired prediction noise "typically exceeds" paired data noise, so averaging several answers per item "can significantly increase [statistical power](#/glossary/statistical-power)" ([Measuring all the noises of LLM Evals](#/papers/wang2025evalnoise "Measuring all the noises of LLM Evals (2025)") abstract).
- Small item sets break the usual error bars: in simulations, central-limit intervals on small evals usually understate uncertainty, and for paired two-model comparisons every non-Bayesian method tested falls short of nominal coverage at small sizes ([Position](#/papers/bowyer2025clt "Position: Don't Use the CLT in LLM Evals With Fewer Than a Few Hundred Datapoints (2025)") abstract, §3.4).
- Each item costs a rollout: GEPA's authors propose, as future work, a smaller or dynamically chosen validation set ([GEPA](#/papers/agrawal2025gepa "GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning (2026)") §4), the opposite of what selection noise asks.
- More items fix noise but not bias: a checker that accepts wrong answers stays wrong at any sample size ([Weak checkers get exploited](#/challenges/weak_checker_exploitation); our reasoning).

## Verifiable signal
Re-score the chosen method on fresh items never used in selection, with a paired test fixed in advance, against an A/A floor (the same method scored twice). In SQL the fresh items can be certified pairs: an LLM proposer makes them on demand, a counterexample is checked by running two queries after a Determinism pre-check, and no judge adds noise. In the DSL gym they are code-made tasks of its certified families, whose keys the engine computes ([A gym of DSL tasks with answer keys made by code](#/challenges/dsl_gym)). The check is cheap, and sound up to the test's stated error rate (our reasoning); realistic hard pairs are [Sourcing realistic, hard query pairs](#/challenges/query_pair_sourcing)'s problem.
