# s1: Simple test-time scaling

**s1** · EMNLP 2025

Read: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.19393) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.1025)  
Code: [s1](https://github.com/simplescaling/s1)

<p class="notice">An AI-written summary for orientation, not a source: check the original paper before relying on anything here.</p>

## In brief

- Budget forcing: end or extend the model's thinking to control compute.
- A small curated set of reasoning traces for fine-tuning.
- Budget forcing is the "answer now" guard of Thinking-budget control.

## In plain words

Reasoning models such as OpenAI's o1 answer better when they think longer, but o1's method is unpublished, and the authors say no replication had openly shown accuracy rising with thinking time (§1). They ask for the simplest recipe that gets both strong reasoning and that rise. They fine-tune an open 32-billion-parameter model on just 1,000 hard, varied math and science questions, each with a reasoning trace written by Google's Gemini thinking model. At answer time they add budget forcing: cut the thinking off at a limit, or, when the model tries to stop, block the stop and append "Wait" so it keeps checking its work. With budget forcing their model goes from 50% to 57% on the 2024 AIME math competition, and beats OpenAI's o1-preview on competition math by up to 27% (abstract). They call it "the first reproduction of OpenAI's test-time scaling curves" (§6.1).

## Background and terms

**Terms to know:** [reinforcement learning](#/glossary/reinforcement-learning) (how o1 and DeepSeek R1 were trained, §1); other field terms are defined below.

**The paper's own terms**
- **test-time scaling**: spending more compute when answering to get better results (§1). **Sequential** scaling builds later computation on earlier (one longer trace); **parallel** scaling combines independent attempts, e.g. by majority voting (§3.1).
- **thinking tokens, end-of-thinking delimiter**: the model reasons between a "think" and an "answer" marker; the delimiter ends thinking (App. D). Compute is counted in thinking tokens (§3.2).
- **budget forcing** (§3.1): to enforce a maximum, append the end-of-thinking delimiter (optionally "Final Answer:") so the model answers now; to enforce a minimum, suppress the delimiter and optionally append "Wait". 2x "Wait" means the stop is suppressed twice (Tab. 4).
- **s1K, s1-32B**: the 1,000-question training set and the model fine-tuned on it (§2, §4.1). **s1K-1.1, s1.1**: the same questions with traces regenerated by DeepSeek r1, and its model (App. A).
- **Control, Scaling, Performance** (§3.2, Eq. 1–3): over runs with different budgets, Control is the share whose thinking stays within the set limits (usually only a maximum); Scaling is the average slope of accuracy against thinking tokens over all pairs of runs; Performance is the best accuracy reached.
- **token-, step- and class-conditional control**: baselines that say in the prompt how long to think: at most N tokens, at most N steps (text between blank lines, "around 100 tokens"), or a generic "short" or "long" prompt (§3.1, App. E.1). The token and step variants are trained on matching formats (Fig. 10).
- **rejection sampling**: sampling until a generation fits the budget (§3.1; temperature 1, Fig. 6); an oracle (an idealized reference) that "captures the posterior over responses conditioned on its length" (§3.1), i.e. it shows what the model answers when its thinking happens to be that short.

**Missing glossary terms**
- **Distillation into compact models**: training a model on outputs of a stronger model; here, fine-tuning on Gemini's traces and answers (§1, §2.1).
- **majority voting**: sample several solutions and return the most frequent answer (§6.2).
- **process reward model**: a model that scores each reasoning step, used to guide tree search; an outcome reward model scores only whole solutions (§6.2).
- **paired bootstrap confidence interval**: a range for the score difference between two models, estimated by resampling the questions; entirely below zero means worse (Tab. 2 caption).

**Builds on**
- OpenAI o1, a closed reasoning model whose test-time scaling they set out to reproduce openly (§1; not listed here).
- DeepSeek R1, [DeepSeek-R1](#/papers/deepseek2025reasoning "DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning (2025)"): o1-level via reinforcement learning (§1); its distilled 32B model, fine-tuned on 800K examples, is compared in Tab. 1 and §4.2.
- LIMA, where 1,000 examples sufficed to align a model; they liken their own hypothesis (pretraining already gives the reasoning ability; fine-tuning activates it) to its "Superficial Alignment Hypothesis" (§6.1; not listed here).
- [compute-optimal test-time scaling](#/papers/snell2024scaling "Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters (2024)") (with [Large Language Monkeys](#/papers/brown2024monkeys "Large Language Monkeys: Scaling Inference Compute with Repeated Sampling (2024)")), for the sequential/parallel split (§3.1).

## Problem and setting

- **Question:** "what is the simplest approach to achieve both test-time scaling and strong reasoning performance?" (§1).
- **Model:** Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, an open instruction-tuned model, fine-tuned in 26 minutes on 16 H100 GPUs (§4.1, App. D).
- **Data correctness:** "Some distilled generations are incorrect", which the authors allow; their grader judges 53.6% of s1K correct (§2.2).
- **Benchmarks** (§4.1): AIME24 (the 30 problems of the 2024 American Invitational Mathematics Examination), MATH500 (500 competition math problems chosen by OpenAI) and GPQA Diamond (198 PhD-level science questions). Training samples sharing an 8-gram (a run of eight consecutive units of text) with them are removed (§2.1, App. C.5).
- **Evaluation:** lm-evaluation-harness (an open evaluation framework), temperature 0 (greedy), accuracy "equivalent to pass@1", one attempt per question (§4.1).
- **Compared models** (§4.1): the closed o1 series; DeepSeek r1 and r1-distill; QwQ-32B-preview (Qwen's open reasoning model); Sky-T1 and Bespoke-32B (fine-tuned on 17K traces from QwQ and r1); Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking (AIME24 only).

## Approach

- **Data pipeline (§2, App. C)**: 59,029 questions from 16 sources (Tab. 7), mostly math problem sets, plus two new sets of Stanford statistics PhD-exam probability questions (s1-prob) and quant-interview brain-teasers (s1-teasers). Gemini Flash Thinking answers each; three filters follow.
  - **Quality:** drop API errors and badly formatted samples; take 384 directly from datasets the authors "perceive as high-quality" (§2.2).
  - **Difficulty:** drop questions that Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct solves, judged by Claude 3.5 Sonnet against the reference; longer traces count as harder.
  - **Diversity:** sort questions into domains of the Mathematics Subject Classification (the American Mathematical Society's topic taxonomy), then repeatedly pick a domain at random and draw a question favoring long traces, until 1,000 across 50 domains (Alg. 1).
- **Baselines (§3.1, App. E.1):** token- and step-conditional control, each also with budget forcing as a hard stop; class-conditional control; rejection sampling.
- **Parallel add-ons (§6.2):** majority voting, and REBASE, a tree search guided by a process reward model (Wu et al. 2024), aggregated by majority voting.

## Results

- **Main comparison (Tab. 1):** on AIME24 / MATH500 / GPQA, s1-32B with budget forcing scores 56.7 / 93.0 / 59.6, against 50.0 / 92.6 / 56.6 without it, 26.7 / 84.0 / 49.0 for the base model and 44.6 / 85.5 / 73.3 for o1-preview. The authors call it "the most sample-efficient reasoning model" (§1); r1-distill is stronger but trained on 800K examples (§4.2).
- **Scaling curves (Fig. 1, Fig. 4(a)):** accuracy rises with budget-forced thinking and eventually flattens (§4.2). Majority voting over the base model does not catch up, which the authors read as sequential beating parallel scaling (Fig. 4(b)).
- **Data ablations (Tab. 2, §5.1):** each 1K variant keeps one criterion only (random after the quality filter, diversity, or longest traces). On AIME24, 1K-random scores 36.7, 1K-diverse 26.7 and 1K-longest 33.3, against 50.0 for s1K; all 59K give 53.3 at 394 H100 GPU hours against 7.
- **Control methods (Tab. 3, §5.2):** on AIME24, Control is 100% for budget forcing and rejection sampling, against 40% (token), 60% (step) and 50% (class-conditional); budget forcing has the best Performance. Token-conditional control and rejection sampling get negative Scaling.
  - The model "cannot reliably count tokens - even when trained to do so"; under step limits, "the model learns to hack its way around the compute constraint" (§5.2).
  - Class-conditional control "can work" (§5.2).
- **Extension string (Tab. 4, §5.2):** "Wait" "generally gives the best performance" against no string, "Alternatively" and "Hmm": it leads on AIME24 and ties the best on MATH500 and GPQA.
- **Rejection sampling (Fig. 6):** longer budgets give lower accuracy; the authors hypothesize that long samples are those where the model erred and backtracked (§5.2).
- **Parallel add-ons (Fig. 7, §6.2):** REBASE "scales better than majority voting, and even sequential scaling in this scenario"; prompted for up to 512 steps, the model overran the context window on 12 of 30 questions.
- **s1.1 (App. A, Tab. 5):** traces from DeepSeek r1 make the model "significantly better than s1".

## Limits the authors state

- Budget forcing "eventually flattens out (Figure 4), and the context window of the underlying language model constrains it" (§6.2); suppressing the stop "too often can lead the model into repetitive loops instead of continued reasoning" (§4.2).
- Filtering with the Qwen models "may be optimized for our setup" (§2.2).
- With vLLM (the inference engine they use), "even when using the same random seeds and greedy sampling, evaluation scores can change significantly across runs" (App. B).
- Class-conditional control "might not scale to finer-grained classes" (App. E.1).
- REBASE "requires an additional forward pass at each step for the reward model" (§6.2).

## Open problems and building blocks

  - "It is an open question whether one can achieve their performance with just 1,000 samples" (r1-distill's, §4.2).
  - Better extrapolation (more compute than the model spends unforced): rotating through different strings, not only "Wait", or frequency penalties or higher temperature against loops (§6.2).
  - Whether budget forcing on a model trained with reinforcement learning "yields better extrapolation" (§6.2).
  - Parallel methods as "an avenue for scaling test-time compute even further; beyond fixed context windows" (§6.2).
- **Released:** "Our model, data, and code are open-source" (abstract); s1.1 was released a week later (App. A).
- **To reuse it:** a 32B instruction-tuned model; traces from a stronger thinking model; a training sequence length that cuts no samples (App. D.1); decoding that can suppress the end-of-thinking token and append text (§3.1).
- **Beyond its domain:** "the idea of model-based filtering generalizes to other setups" (§2.2).
