## Papers

- **[Faithful Autoformalization via Roundtrip Verification and Repair](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25031 "Faithful Autoformalization via Roundtrip Verification and Repair (2026)")** (preprint 2026). Checks an LLM's translation of a natural-language rule into logic with no reference translation: formalize, translate back to English, formalize again, and ask an SMT solver whether the two formalizations are equivalent; when they are not, an LLM judge names the first translation step that lost meaning and only that step is repaired (abstract; §3–§4, Alg. 1). <span class="links">[🔎](#/papers/amrollahi2026roundtrip "Summary") · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25031) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25031) · [Code](https://github.com/daneshvar-amrollahi/roundtrip-autoformalization)</span> <span class="tags"><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/harness-general">harness-general</a><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/llm-assist">llm-assist</a><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/llm-method">llm-method</a><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/smt-misc">smt-misc</a></span>
- **[SQLRepair](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.05729 "SQLRepair: Identifying and Repairing Mistakes in Student-Authored SQL Queries (2021)")**: Identifying and Repairing Mistakes in Student-Authored SQL Queries (ICSE-SEET 2021). Collects the SQL queries that undergraduates wrote for programming-by-example problems, each given as (source, destination) table pairs that a query must turn one into the other, and classifies their syntax and semantic errors (abstract; §II; §IV-A). <span class="links">[🔎](#/papers/preslermarshall2021sqlrepair "Summary") · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.05729) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05729) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE-SEET52601.2021.00030) · [Code](https://github.com/kpresler/SQLRepair)</span> <span class="tags"><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/smt-misc">smt-misc</a></span>
- **[Know Your Limits](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16118 "Know Your Limits: On the Faithfulness of LLMs as Solvers and Autoformalizers in Legal Reasoning (2026)")**: On the Faithfulness of LLMs as Solvers and Autoformalizers in Legal Reasoning ((LLMs as solvers and autoformalizers in legal reasoning), preprint 2026 (accepted by the AI4Law and AI4Math workshops at ICML)). Compares three ways of deciding whether a contract clause entails, contradicts or is neutral to a hypothesis: the LLM classifies the text directly, the LLM classifies after autoformalizing both into first-order logic, and the Z3 SMT solver decides over that formalization; on a hand re-annotated subset of ContractNLI, with five LLMs (abstract; §1; §3). <span class="links">[🔎](#/papers/wang2026limits "Summary") · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16118) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16118)</span> <span class="tags"><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/harness-general">harness-general</a><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/llm-assist">llm-assist</a><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/llm-method">llm-method</a><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/smt-misc">smt-misc</a></span>

"Weak equivalence": non-equivalent queries that agree on the *current* database; a Query Lattice caches OLAP results. <span class="links">[🔎](#/papers/you2025weakeq "Summary") · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/icde65448.2025.00144)</span> <span class="tags"><a class="tag sub" href="#/tags/smt-misc">smt-misc</a></span>
